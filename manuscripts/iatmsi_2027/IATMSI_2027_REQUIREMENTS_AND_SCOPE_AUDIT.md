**Purpose:** Establish an evidence-based publication plan for IATMSI-2027 without changing experimental code or treating conference website claims as guarantees.

## 1. Executive finding

The COVID respiratory-audio study is directly within **Track 1: Innovative Solutions for Healthcare, Biomedical Engineering and Sustainable Development**. The official call explicitly includes AI in healthcare, digital health, and data/AI approaches to COVID-19 challenges. Track 3 is a secondary technical fit, but Track 1 is the clearer submission category.

The initial paper should be designed as a **six-page IEEE double-column conference paper**. The official submission and camera-ready pages both state a six-page limit. Although the conference FAQ also mentions up to two paid extra pages, this conflicts with the repeated strict-six-page wording and should not be relied upon without written confirmation from the 2027 publication chairs.

The complete project is too broad for a coherent six-page paper. The best IATMSI submission is therefore not a compressed inventory of every experiment. It should answer one focused question:

> **How much does the measured performance of a strong COVID-19 respiratory-audio model change when evaluation moves from conventional participant-disjoint testing to temporally constrained and independent cross-dataset testing?**

The unrestricted master manuscript should remain the scientific source of truth. The conference paper should select only the evidence necessary to answer the focused question, while preserving all claim boundaries: COUGHVID externally evaluates only the cough branch, not full cough-breath-speech fusion; unfinished HST runs must not be reported; and IEEE Xplore or Scopus inclusion must not be stated as guaranteed.

## 4. IEEE-wide writing and ethics requirements relevant to this paper

| Area | Official IEEE guidance | Consequence for this manuscript |
|---|---|---|
| Title | Specific, concise, descriptive; avoid empty words such as “new” or “novel” | Name the task and evaluation question. Do not use “novel framework” unless a genuine new framework is formally defined and evaluated. |
| Abstract | One self-contained paragraph, no more than 250 words, with conclusions and implications; no references, equations, footnotes, or undefined abbreviations; include 3-5 keywords | Draft the abstract last from final results. Define COVID-19 and any model acronym on first use. |
| Introduction | Establish state of research, exact gap/question, motivation, and contribution | Use only the literature necessary to establish validation-protocol sensitivity and transportability as the gap. |
| Methods | Sufficient detail for replication, with relevant definitions and equations | Report participant grouping, calendar split, feature selection location, model-selection boundary, thresholds, and uncertainty procedure. Omit code filenames. |
| Results/discussion | Report and interpret results, acknowledge limitations, avoid exaggeration | Separate internal multimodal results from cough-only external transfer. Do not equate near-chance external discrimination with proof that no biological signal exists. |
| Human-subject data | Include IRB/ethics oversight or explain why review was not conducted; report consent or explain why it was not obtained | Document that the work is secondary analysis of public datasets and accurately reproduce each dataset's consent/ethics statement. Verify wording from original dataset publications before submission. |
| Originality | No concurrent refereed submission; cite and distinguish related prior work | The unrestricted master may later become a journal extension, but it must cite the conference paper and clearly identify substantial added technical content. |

## 6. Recommended unrestricted master manuscript

The unrestricted master should be maintained first as the complete, internally consistent research record. It is not bound to the IATMSI page limit and should include all completed evidence needed for a later journal submission.

### 6.1 Scientific question

How reliable are COVID-19 respiratory-audio models when the evaluation explicitly tests participant independence, calendar drift, metadata shortcuts, calibration, and transfer to a separately collected dataset?

### 6.2 Evidence families to preserve

1. **Cohort and label construction:** Coswara development data, COUGHVID external cough data, label provenance, inclusion/exclusion, missingness, class balance, and modality availability.
2. **Audio processing:** signal loading, resampling, quality checks, segment construction, and all representation families actually evaluated.
3. **Conventional acoustic representations:** ComParE 2016 and IS10 summaries, feature merge, training-only feature ranking, and selected top-800 feature bank.
4. **Deep representations:** WavLM, CNN-BiGRU, and HST only where runs are complete and auditable.
5. **Model selection:** training/validation/test boundaries, imbalance handling, threshold selection, and the exact rule selecting the final model or fusion.
6. **Multimodal modeling:** modality-specific predictions and validation-learned or fixed fusion, with the weights and selection procedure reported.
7. **Validation ladder:** participant-disjoint internal split, time-stratified split, strict early-to-late evaluation, reverse temporal analysis, and multi-seed stability.
8. **External transfer:** cough-to-cough transfer from Coswara to COUGHVID, explicitly not full multimodal transfer.
9. **Uncertainty and calibration:** bootstrap confidence intervals, paired tests where pairing exists, calibration summaries, fixed-sensitivity operating points, and decision curves.
10. **Shortcut/confounding analyses:** metadata-only prediction, label-shuffle controls, permutation importance, IPW or matched-cohort sensitivity, recording protocol and duration checks.
11. **Shift mechanisms:** source-target domain discrimination/support diagnostics and early-versus-late feature-selection stability.
12. **Clinical interpretation:** base-rate-aware precision, limitations of threshold recalibration, and decision-utility boundaries.
13. **Incremental value:** metadata-only versus metadata-plus-audio analysis, with alignment/sample-size limitations stated exactly.
14. **Equity and subgroup evidence:** included as exploratory unless the analysis plan and multiplicity control support confirmatory claims.

### 6.3 Master-manuscript claim boundary

The master should support a reliability/transportability contribution. It should not claim that:

- the study proves the absence of a biological COVID-19 audio signal;
- COUGHVID validates breath, speech, or full multimodal fusion;
- poor external transfer alone proves shortcut learning;
- an architecture is state of the art without a protocol-identical comparison and uncertainty;
- recalibration can change AUROC;
- a model is clinically deployable from retrospective public-dataset evidence.

## 7. Recommended six-page IATMSI conference focus

### 7.1 Recommended paper concept

**Working title before the HST decision gate:**

> **Validation-Protocol Sensitivity of Multimodal COVID-19 Respiratory-Audio Screening**

This title is specific and makes no unsupported novelty or superiority claim. If a completed HST experiment becomes the paper's single anchor and is evaluated through the relevant ladder, a model-specific alternative is:

> **Protocol-Sensitive Generalization of Hierarchical Spectrogram Transformers for COVID-19 Respiratory-Audio Screening**

Do not use the HST-specific title if HST has only an internal result or lacks the temporal/external evaluations claimed by the paper.

### 7.2 Conference research question

Does the apparent performance of one selected strong respiratory-audio pipeline persist when evaluation moves from conventional internal testing to calendar-aware and independent cross-dataset testing?

### 7.3 Three contribution claims that fit six pages

1. **Controlled comparison across evaluation protocols.** Apply one frozen pipeline under conventional participant-disjoint, time-aware, and strict chronological evaluation rather than comparing unrelated models across protocols.
2. **Matched external portability test.** Evaluate the cough branch on COUGHVID using source-trained preprocessing, features/model, and threshold; identify this as cough-only transfer.
3. **Mechanistic reliability evidence.** Use one compact mechanism result, preferably temporal feature instability or recording-protocol permutation importance, to explain why internal performance may not transport.

Uncertainty intervals should accompany all principal performance differences. Calibration or clinical operating points may be included only if they fit without displacing the core methods needed to understand the comparison.

### 7.5 Results decision gate

Use this gate after the ongoing HST work is complete:

| HST evidence available | IATMSI action |
|---|---|
| HST has complete internal, time-aware/temporal, and appropriately matched external results | Use HST as the single main model and one conventional comparator. Center the paper on protocol sensitivity. |
| HST has internal and temporal results but no legitimate external counterpart | Use HST for the internal/temporal ladder; present the existing cough-only external transfer as a separate cross-architecture robustness result, not as direct HST transfer. |
| HST has only internal results | Do not make HST the headline. Use the completed ComParE+IS10/fusion pipeline and deep-transfer comparators already present. |
| HST fails, is unstable, or remains incomplete | Report it only in the master manuscript if methodologically interpretable. Do not include an unfinished run in the conference paper. |

### 7.7 Recommended visual plan

Because prior-paper PDFs were inaccessible, this is a manuscript-design recommendation rather than an observed venue statistic.

1. **Figure 1: Study and evaluation design.** Show Coswara modalities, frozen training pipeline, participant-disjoint split, calendar-aware split, early-to-late split, and cough-only transfer to COUGHVID. Clearly prevent the visual from implying multimodal external validation.
2. **Figure 2: Protocol performance ladder with 95% confidence intervals.** Place internal, time-aware, temporal, and external cough results in one readable plot. Do not put incomparable multimodal and cough-only points on an unlabeled single line.
3. **Figure 3 or compact panel: one mechanism.** Choose either early/late feature overlap or grouped metadata permutation importance. Do not add both if readability suffers.
4. **Table I: Cohort and protocol matrix.** Participants/recordings, positives, modalities, split rule, and threshold source.
5. **Table II: Principal results.** AUROC, AUPRC, balanced accuracy, calibration metric if central, sample size, and confidence interval.

If space is tight, merge Figures 2 and 3 as labeled panels rather than shrinking text below readable size.

### 7.8 What to leave out

- script names, repository paths, log filenames, and artifact-generation narration;
- every attempted architecture or optimizer;
- claims that another paper's metrics are fabricated or dishonest;
- an apples-to-oranges leaderboard mixing datasets, labels, modalities, and splits;
- decorative pipeline diagrams without experimental meaning;
- Grad-CAM unless a spectrogram model is actually central and the explanation is validated;
- detailed parameter grids that do not affect reproducibility of the selected final run;
- claims of “clinical utility” unless decision consequences and prevalence are analyzed for that specific endpoint;
- a promise of supplementary material, because the inspected IATMSI pages do not define a supplementary-file policy.

## 8. Comparison strategy for related papers

The six-page paper should use two distinct comparison tables or table blocks:

### 8.1 Protocol-matched comparison

Compare only studies/runs sharing the relevant dataset, label definition, modality, split unit, number of folds or holdout rule, and metric definition. If any element differs, mark the row as partially matched. A numerical difference under mismatched protocols is descriptive, not evidence that one pipeline is superior.

### 8.2 Context-only literature comparison

Use a compact matrix with columns for dataset, modality, participant independence, temporal evaluation, external dataset, and reported metric. This demonstrates what each paper evaluated. Do not rank models across incompatible protocols.

The conference paper can state that its contribution is the controlled change in evaluation conditions for a frozen pipeline. It should not accuse earlier authors of concealment unless an exact quotation and protocol evidence support the statement.

## 11. Bottom-line recommendation

Maintain two documents with different jobs:

1. **Unrestricted master manuscript:** complete evidence record and later journal foundation.
2. **IATMSI-2027 six-page paper:** one controlled protocol-sensitivity study anchored by one completed final model, one matched external test, one mechanism analysis, and uncertainty.

This division is both scientifically cleaner and compatible with IEEE's publication-evolution policy, provided the conference paper is cited and the later journal manuscript clearly identifies its substantial additional technical content.
