\begin{abstract}
Respiratory-audio studies can collect several prompted sounds from each person,
which makes participant grouping and separate fitting, selection, and test roles
essential. We evaluated an audio-only system in 2,088 quality-passing Coswara
participants assigned to fixed, participant-disjoint training, validation, and
test sets. Each recording was represented by \CandidateFeatureCount{} acoustic
variables. Gain-based ranking fitted only to training data retained
\SelectedFeatureCount{} variables. Validation data selected the classifier for
each sound, the sound combination, and the operating threshold. The selected
  equal-weight cough--speech probability mean achieved an area under the receiver
  operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of \FusionTestAUROC{} (95\% participant-bootstrap
confidence interval \FusionAUCILow{}--\FusionAUCIHigh{}) and an area under the
precision--recall curve of \FusionTestAUPRC{} (95\% confidence interval
\FusionAUPRCILow{}--\FusionAUPRCIHigh{}) on \FusionTestN{} held-out participants.
  Fusion improved AUROC over cough by \FusionMinusCoughAUROC{} (95\% DeLong confidence interval
\FusionMinusCoughAUCILow{}--\FusionMinusCoughAUCIHigh{}), while its
\FusionMinusSpeechAUROC{} difference from speech was inconclusive (95\%
confidence interval \FusionMinusSpeechAUCILow{}--\FusionMinusSpeechAUCIHigh{},
$p=\FusionMinusSpeechAUROCPValue$). Thus, speech added information beyond cough,
but cough did not establish a gain beyond speech on these held-out participants.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Smartphone cough, breathing, and speech recordings have been studied as
accessible inputs for COVID-19 risk assessment. Yet public resources and
experimental protocols differ substantially \cite{aleixandre2022review}.
COVID-19 Sounds contains cough, breath, and voice \cite{xia2021covid}, COUGHVID
is cough centred \cite{orlandic2021coughvid}, and Coswara records nine prompted
respiratory and vocal tasks with participant metadata
\cite{bhattacharya2023coswara}. Accordingly, \emph{multimodal} may denote
multiple sound tasks, multiple learned representations, or audio combined with
non-audio variables. We use \emph{multisound} for the first meaning and use no
symptoms or demographic variables as model inputs.

Multiple tasks may carry complementary information because a forced cough,
sustained phonation, and counting speech excite different production
mechanisms. Coswara studies have used transfer learning across cough, breath,
and speech \cite{pahar2022transfer}, fused acoustic categories and symptoms
\cite{chetupalli2023multimodal}, and combined waveform and spectrogram
representations across several recordings \cite{truong2024fair}. More inputs do
not guarantee a better model. An added task also increases missingness,
dimensionality, and opportunities for model selection. Its value must be judged
on participants not used to fit the sound branches. A fused score alone cannot
show whether the combination improves on cough, on the strongest single-sound
branch, on both, or on neither.

Evaluation design is equally important. Participant allocation and cohort
composition can alter respiratory-audio estimates \cite{han2022realistic}, and
matching recruitment channel, demographics, and symptoms can reduce apparent
performance \cite{coppock2024audio}. Deployment and longitudinal studies also
show sensitivity to acquisition setting and calendar period
\cite{grant2022deployment,kim2024longitudinal,ganitidis2025drift}. These findings
do not preclude internal development, but they require separate data for
fitting, choosing a system, and estimating its performance.

This study contributes a participant-disjoint path from training-only feature
ranking to validation-only branch, sound-set, and threshold selection.
Participant-level score fusion keeps branch contribution and missing-task sample
counts explicit. Paired held-out intervals then distinguish improvement over
cough from improvement over the strongest single-sound branch. These data-role
boundaries keep final outcomes outside system selection.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Datasets and Benchmark Tasks}

DiCOVA supplied a common blind-test benchmark with cough and multisound tracks
\cite{muguli2021dicova}. Coswara's repeated tasks are useful for
participant-aligned fusion, but recording-level random allocation could place
different recordings from one person in different data roles. A meaningful
comparison must specify the
dataset snapshot, label rule, sound tasks, split unit, and selection protocol.

COUGHVID is centred on cough recordings \cite{orlandic2021coughvid}, whereas
COVID-19 Sounds and Coswara include several prompted sound tasks
\cite{xia2021covid,bhattacharya2023coswara}. These resources consequently
support different estimands: a cough classifier, a multisound classifier, or a
system that also receives symptoms. We restrict the present question to
audio-only, participant-aligned sound fusion within Coswara. This avoids
crediting non-audio predictors to the acoustic system and permits every sound
combination to be evaluated on a common participant identity.

\subsection{Representations and Fusion}

Model families span ResNet cough systems \cite{laguarta2020cough}, transfer
learning for smartphone cough and multiple Coswara tasks
\cite{pahar2021smartphone,pahar2022transfer}, convolutional cough--breath--voice
models \cite{celik2023covidcoughnet}, and hierarchical spectrogram transformers
\cite{aytekin2024hst}. Engineered descriptors remain relevant in modest cohorts:
ranked acoustic ensembles \cite{chowdhury2022mcdm} and a ComParE-SVM/log-mel
CNN fusion \cite{avila2021feature} have both been evaluated on respiratory
audio. Fusion itself may combine features before classification or probabilities
after classification \cite{baltrusaitis2019survey}. Chetupalli \emph{et al.}
reported 0.880 AUC from three acoustic categories and 0.963 after adding
symptoms \cite{chetupalli2023multimodal}. FAIR reported
$0.8658\pm0.0115$ AUC by attending over wav2vec and DeiT representations from
seven recordings \cite{truong2024fair}. We use score-level fusion because its
branches and sound contribution remain directly inspectable.

\subsection{Evaluation Practice}

Evaluation can dominate apparent model quality. Nonnested development and blind
evaluation can diverge \cite{avila2021feature}, while participant composition
and cohort matching alter the target question
\cite{han2022realistic,coppock2024audio}. In line with TRIPOD+AI reporting
principles \cite{collins2024tripod}, we state the independent unit, permitted
role of each partition, selection source, threshold source, and uncertainty
target. Published values are treated as protocol-qualified context rather than
an interchangeable leaderboard.

\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Cohort, Labels, and Partitioning}

The indexed Coswara release contained 2,746 participants. We retained 2,114 after
mapping positive-status tokens to the positive class and negative, healthy,
normal, or control tokens to the negative class. Other values were excluded.
Quality screening left
2,088 participants with at least one eligible cough, breathing, or speech
recording. A participant could contribute two coughs, two breathing recordings,
two counting recordings, and three sustained vowels. All recordings from a
participant remained in one fixed partition. The 70/15/15 assignment used seed
42 and a global stratification hierarchy: label--age-band--gender when feasible,
then label--age-band, and finally label. Table~\ref{tab:cohort}
reports the quality-passing analysis cohort. The participant, rather than a
recording or sound segment, was the independent evaluation unit.

\subsection{Audio Quality Control and Preprocessing}

Recordings were decoded as mono waveforms and resampled to 16~kHz. Silence was
trimmed at 35~dB below peak energy. A root-mean-square detector used 25-ms
frames and a 10-ms hop. Frames above 25\% of the recording's maximum RMS
defined the active interval, expanded by a 120-ms margin. Peak normalization
then preceded centered cropping or zero padding to 4~s for cough and 5~s for
breathing and speech. A recording was eligible when its duration was at least
0.5~s for cough or 1~s for breathing/speech, its silence ratio did not exceed
0.70, and its clipping ratio did not exceed 0.01. These rules were fixed before
the reported fusion selection. No validation or test waveform was augmented.

\subsection{Acoustic Representation and Feature Ranking}

Three feature sources were merged by recording identifier. openSMILE ComParE
2016 and IS10 supplied \CompareFeatureCount{} and \ISFeatureCount{} standard
functionals covering energy, spectral shape, cepstral trajectories, and voicing
\cite{eyben2010opensmile,schuller2010is10,schuller2016compare}. A
\ProjectFeatureCount{}-variable project block summarized 40 MFCC trajectories
and their differences, 64 log-mel bands, chroma, spectral contrast, tonnetz,
energy, zero crossing, spectral shape, tempo, and timing. Four prefixed
active-interval variables from each openSMILE extraction added
\OpenSmileEventFeatureCount{} further columns. The resulting bank contained
\CandidateFeatureCount{} numeric candidates per recording. Timing descriptors
can reflect recording behaviour and are not interpreted as biological markers.

Ranking used only training rows. A LightGBM ranker was fitted separately in
each sound modality. If $g_{jm}$ is the gain of feature $j$ in modality $m$,
the common score was
\begin{equation}
s_j=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}
\frac{g_{jm}}{\max_k g_{km}}.
\label{eq:rank}
\end{equation}
The max normalization prevents one modality's gain scale from dominating the
aggregate. The \SelectedFeatureCount{} highest-scoring names were frozen for
validation and test. Feature budgets of 500, 800, and 1,200 were explored
during earlier development. The 800-feature configuration was carried forward
before this reporting analysis and was not chosen from the present test row.
Within each model pipeline, constant variables were removed
and an ANOVA F-score retained 80\% of candidates for boosted models or 60\%
for the radial-basis SVC.

\subsection{Modality Models and Validation-Guided Fusion}

Four classifier families formed the final candidate bank: LightGBM, XGBoost,
CatBoost, and a sigmoid-calibrated radial-basis SVC
\cite{ke2017lightgbm,chen2016xgboost,prokhorenkova2018catboost,cortes1995svm}.
LightGBM used 900 trees, learning rate 0.025, 31 leaves, minimum child size 20,
row and feature subsampling of 0.85, and $L_2$ regularization 2. XGBoost used
800 depth-3 trees, learning rate 0.025, row and feature subsampling of 0.85,
minimum child weight 2, and $L_2$ regularization 2. CatBoost used 900 depth-5
iterations and learning rate 0.025. The SVC used $C=2$, class-balanced fitting,
scaled inputs, and three-fold sigmoid calibration
\cite{niculescu2005calibration}. For the boosted models, SMOTE with three
nearest neighbors was applied inside the training pipeline after feature
filtering. Validation and test data were never resampled \cite{chawla2002smote}.
All stochastic components used seed 42. SVC calibration folds operated on
training rows. Repeated recordings could occur across these internal folds, but
no validation or test row entered calibration.

The candidate bank was heterogeneous but finite. The three gradient-boosting
implementations test related nonlinear tree learners with different
regularization and tree-construction rules. The calibrated SVC supplies a
non-tree, margin-based comparator. No family was selected from test
performance. For each sound, validation compared each candidate with an equal
probability mean of all four. Thus, ``four-model mean'' denotes a fixed
arithmetic average rather than learned or manually assigned model weights.
This hierarchy separates classifier choice within a sound, sound-set choice
across modalities, and operating-threshold choice.

Recording probabilities were averaged first within participant and modality.
For each modality, validation AUROC, with AUPRC as the tie breaker, selected
either one classifier or the equal mean of all four classifier probabilities.
Let $p_m(x)$ denote the selected probability for modality $m$ and participant
$x$. The primary fusion rule for a modality set $C$ was the
uniform mean
\begin{equation}
p_C(x)=\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{m\in C}p_m(x).
\label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
Validation AUROC, with AUPRC as the tie breaker, selected among the four
combinations of two or three sound modalities. A validation-AUPRC-weighted mean and a class-balanced
logistic stack fitted to validation probabilities were retained as exploratory
sensitivity analyses. Neither was eligible to replace the uniform primary
rule. For weighted fusion, $a_m=\max(\mathrm{AUPRC}_m-0.5,0.01)$ and
$w_m=a_m/\sum_{k\in C}a_k$, using validation AUPRC. The resulting cough and
speech weights were \CoughFusionWeight{} and \SpeechFusionWeight{}. The probability
threshold maximizing validation balanced accuracy (\FusionThreshold{}) was
then frozen for test balanced accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and F1.

\subsection{Leakage Controls and Audit Trail}

The data-role boundary was enforced at every supervised step. Split assignment
preceded feature ranking. Constant removal, ANOVA filtering, scaling,
calibration, and SMOTE were fitted without validation or test rows. Model and
modality selection used validation outcomes only. Within the reported analysis,
test outcomes were not inputs to feature ranking, branch selection, sound-set
selection, weighting, or threshold choice. Selecting features or fusion rules
on final outcomes would turn the test value into another development estimate.

Missing sound tasks were not replaced by another participant's recording.
Single-modality metrics used all eligible participants for that modality,
whereas a fusion row used the intersection with valid predictions for every
member of its modality set. Sample size is reported separately for each row.
Split assignments, selected feature names, model-selection records,
thresholds, and aggregate evidence tables were retained as machine-readable
artifacts so that each reported number can be traced to its data role.

\subsection{Outcomes and Uncertainty}

The primary endpoint was participant-level AUROC. AUPRC addressed the less
frequent positive class. At the frozen threshold, balanced accuracy was
$(\mathrm{sensitivity}+\mathrm{specificity})/2$, and
$F_1=2(\mathrm{precision}\times\mathrm{recall})/
(\mathrm{precision}+\mathrm{recall})$. The AUROC intervals in
Fig.~\ref{fig:selection-results}(b) and the primary-fusion AUPRC interval used
1,000 nonparametric participant resamples of each test row. They quantify
sampling uncertainty for a fixed system and cohort, not refitting variation.
Fusion was also compared with each
constituent branch on the same complete-case participants. AUROC differences
used paired DeLong intervals \cite{delong1988correlated}, with Holm correction
across the two branch comparisons. AUPRC differences used 2,000 paired
participant resamples.

\subsection{Feature and Validation Selection}

The retained bank contained \SelectedCompareFeatureCount{} ComParE variables,
\SelectedISFeatureCount{} IS10 variables, \SelectedProjectFeatureCount{}
signal-summary variables, and all \SelectedEventFeatureCount{} active-interval
variables (Table~\ref{tab:features}). Thus, the common rank did not collapse to
one extractor: the retained proportions were 6.2\%, 7.1\%, and 13.0\% for the
three acoustic blocks. Retention of every timing variable is also a caution:
those variables can encode how a task was performed or recorded, so their
selection is predictive evidence, not physiological interpretation.

Validation AUROC selected the four-model probability mean for breathing
(\BreathValidationAUROC{}) and cough (\CoughValidationAUROC{}), and LightGBM
for speech (\SpeechValidationAUROC{}). Among the uniform combinations,
cough plus speech ranked first (AUROC \FusionValidationAUROC{}, AUPRC
\FusionValidationAUPRC{}), narrowly above cough plus breathing plus speech
(AUROC \AllModalitiesValidationAUROC{}, AUPRC
\AllModalitiesValidationAUPRC{}). Breathing plus speech reached
\BreathSpeechValidationAUROC{}/\BreathSpeechValidationAUPRC{}, and cough plus
breathing reached \CoughBreathValidationAUROC{}/
\CoughBreathValidationAUPRC{} (AUROC/AUPRC). This ordering fixed cough plus
speech before the matching test row was retrieved.

The selection path also preserved sample accounting. Candidate branch metrics
were computed after averaging all eligible recordings belonging to the same
participant. Combination metrics then used only participants with predictions
for every included sound. Validation sample sizes were 311 for cough, 311 for
speech, and 310 for cough plus speech. The corresponding held-out test sizes were
316, 314, and 314. These one- or two-person differences arise from missing or
quality-ineligible sound tasks, not from post-outcome filtering.

Across the 15 branch candidates, breathing selected the four-model mean at
0.783 validation AUROC. For cough, the mean and RBF-SVC tied at 0.806554.
AUPRC selected the mean (0.752 versus 0.737). Speech selected LightGBM at
0.843 AUROC, 0.009 above the four-model mean. Across 500, 800, and 1,200 retained
features, best validation AUROC varied only from 0.780 to 0.786 for breathing,
0.813 to 0.815 for cough, and 0.836 to 0.845 for speech. The carried-forward
800-feature budget was therefore not an isolated validation optimum.

\subsection{Held-Out Participant Performance}

Table~\ref{tab:results} presents the selected branches and fusion sensitivity
analyses. Speech was the strongest selected single modality with AUROC
\SpeechTestAUROC{} and AUPRC \SpeechTestAUPRC{}. The primary cough--speech
uniform mean yielded AUROC \FusionTestAUROC{} (95\% CI
\FusionAUCILow{}--\FusionAUCIHigh{}), AUPRC \FusionTestAUPRC{} (95\% CI
\FusionAUPRCILow{}--\FusionAUPRCIHigh{}), balanced accuracy
\FusionTestBalancedAccuracy{}, and F1 \FusionTestFOne{} on
\FusionTestN{} complete-case participants. On these same participants, fusion
exceeded cough by \FusionMinusCoughAUROC{} AUROC (95\% paired DeLong CI
\FusionMinusCoughAUCILow{}--\FusionMinusCoughAUCIHigh{}, Holm-adjusted
$p=\FusionMinusCoughAUROCHolmPValue$) and \FusionMinusCoughAUPRC{} AUPRC
(95\% paired-bootstrap CI \FusionMinusCoughAUPRCILow{}--
\FusionMinusCoughAUPRCIHigh{}). Against speech, the differences were smaller:
\FusionMinusSpeechAUROC{} AUROC (95\% CI \FusionMinusSpeechAUCILow{}--
\FusionMinusSpeechAUCIHigh{}, $p=\FusionMinusSpeechAUROCPValue$) and
\FusionMinusSpeechAUPRC{} AUPRC (95\% CI \FusionMinusSpeechAUPRCILow{}--
\FusionMinusSpeechAUPRCIHigh{}). The weighted mean and logistic stack gave
AUROCs \WeightedTestAUROC{} and \StackTestAUROC{}, respectively, but remained
exploratory because the reporting rule did not permit either to replace the
uniform primary fusion.
At the frozen threshold, the primary fusion sensitivity was
\FusionTestSensitivity{} and specificity was \FusionTestSpecificity{}. These
operating-point values were not retuned on the test cohort.

A validation-selected XGBoost feature-concatenation comparator reached
AUROC/AUPRC \FeatureFusionValidationAUROC{}/\FeatureFusionValidationAUPRC{}
on validation and \FeatureFusionTestAUROC{}/\FeatureFusionTestAUPRC{} on test.
The corresponding score-level mean reached \FusionValidationAUROC{}/
\FusionValidationAUPRC{} and \FusionTestAUROC{}/\FusionTestAUPRC{}, descriptive
test differences of \ScoreMinusFeatureAUROC{} AUROC and
\ScoreMinusFeatureAUPRC{} AUPRC. The logistic stack added only 0.002 test
AUROC over the mean while worsening Brier score from \UniformTestBrier{} to
\StackTestBrier{} and ECE from \UniformTestECE{} to \StackTestECE{}.

Repeating training and validation-guided branch selection with random states
42--46, while retaining uniform cough--speech fusion, yielded test AUROC
\MultiSeedAUROCMean{}$\pm$\MultiSeedAUROCSD{} (range
\MultiSeedAUROCLow{}--\MultiSeedAUROCHigh{}) and AUPRC
\MultiSeedAUPRCMean{}$\pm$\MultiSeedAUPRCSD{}. Because all runs used the same
314 test participants, these standard deviations describe fitting and
selection variability, not population-sampling uncertainty.

\subsection{Context within Internal Coswara Studies}

Table~\ref{tab:context} places the result beside the nearest published
internal Coswara estimates. The Coswara data paper combined nine sound tasks
with symptoms. Chetupalli \emph{et al.} fused LSTM mel-spectrogram scores from
breathing, cough, and speech. FAIR used seven sound instances with wav2vec and
DeiT representations. Cohort snapshot, label filtering, inputs, and partition
construction differ, so the values are descriptive protocol context rather
than a statistical ranking.

The closest audio-only comparisons are Chetupalli \emph{et al.} at 0.880 and
FAIR at $0.866\pm0.012$. The 0.915 Coswara data-paper value also receives
symptoms. The present 0.895 estimate is therefore within the range of reported
internal Coswara results without relying on non-audio inputs. It is not a
head-to-head superiority result: no common participant set, label snapshot,
candidate search, or uncertainty calculation was available across studies.
The defensible comparison is that competitive internal discrimination remains
possible under explicit participant and selection boundaries.

\section{Discussion}

Validation selected cough plus speech by only 0.0011 AUROC over all three sound
types. The three-sound alternative had slightly higher validation AUPRC. The
choice is therefore specific to the declared AUROC-first rule, not evidence that
breathing is generally uninformative. The paired test results reveal an
asymmetric contribution: adding speech to cough improved AUROC by
\FusionMinusCoughAUROC{}, whereas adding cough to the stronger speech branch
changed AUROC by only \FusionMinusSpeechAUROC{} and did not establish
superiority. Balanced accuracy was essentially unchanged relative to speech and
F1 was lower, so the discrimination gain did not improve every operating-point
measure.

The score-level design exposes the contribution of each sound. The primary rule
gives cough and speech equal weight. Validation-AUPRC weighting changed these to
\CoughFusionWeight{} and \SpeechFusionWeight{}, while logistic stacking learned
its coefficients from validation probabilities. Both exploratory rules remained
within 0.003 AUROC of the uniform mean, but stacking was less well calibrated.
Score fusion had higher validation and test AUROC/AUPRC than the selected
feature-concatenation comparator, but this contrast is descriptive because
paired uncertainty was not computed. Its defensible advantage is an explicit
branch structure and fixed rule, not established statistical superiority.

The improvement over cough supports incremental value from the speech tasks in
this cohort. It does not prove a physiological mechanism: cough contains
transient respiratory events, while counting and sustained phonation provide
longer voiced segments for cepstral and voicing statistics. The near-null
fusion-versus-speech contrast suggests that speech carried most of the ranking
information. Moreover, selected timing variables may encode task execution or
recording behaviour rather than disease physiology. The claim is therefore
about predictive contribution under a controlled procedure, not a discovered
acoustic biomarker.

The test AUROC lies within the range of the closest internal Coswara studies,
but Table~\ref{tab:context} is not a leaderboard. Chetupalli \emph{et al.}
studied acoustic-category fusion and the additional effect of symptoms
\cite{chetupalli2023multimodal}. FAIR attended over learned waveform and image
representations from seven recordings \cite{truong2024fair}. Here the narrower
question is audio-only probability fusion under explicit train, validation, and
test responsibilities. Participant grouping, training-only fitting and SMOTE,
participant pooling, and a frozen threshold limit common sources of internal
optimism. The bootstrap interval makes the precision of the fixed test estimate
explicit.

\subsection{Limitations}

First, this is a retrospective analysis of one crowdsourced dataset. Labels
were not independently adjudicated by the present authors, and internal
performance does not establish clinical or cross-dataset validity. Second,
validation participants served several development roles: branch selection,
sound-set selection, and threshold choice. The reporting rule was reconstructed
after broader development rather than preregistered, and 800 was one of three
explored feature budgets. Nested outer evaluation would more fully cover this
selection process. Third, repeated recordings were fitted as separate training
rows before participant pooling, and SVC calibration used row-level internal
folds. Although neither crosses final partitions, a grouped hierarchical
learner could model within-person dependence more directly. Fourth, fusion is a complete-case analysis of 314 test
participants. Performance among people unable to provide both selected sounds
was not estimated. Fifth, all eight timing variables survived ranking and may
capture task execution or recording behaviour rather than respiratory state.
Finally, the bootstrap and paired intervals condition on the frozen systems and
test cohort. The five-seed analysis captures some fitting and validation-choice
variation on that same cohort, but neither analysis replaces external or nested
population-level validation.

\section{Conclusion}

Training-only acoustic ranking and validation-guided selection chose a uniform
cough--speech probability mean, which achieved participant-level test AUROC
\FusionTestAUROC{}. On matched participants it improved over cough by
\FusionMinusCoughAUROC{} AUROC, while its \FusionMinusSpeechAUROC{} difference
from speech was inconclusive. The paired analysis shows where fusion adds value:
speech improved the cough branch, but cough did not produce an established gain
over speech. The reported workflow keeps participant identities disjoint and
keeps final outcomes out of feature and system selection. Temporal, external,
and clinical validity remain separate questions.

\section*{Ethics and Data Availability}

This work is a secondary analysis of the public Coswara resource. No new
participants were recruited. Consent, ethics approval, and collection procedures
are documented in the source publication \cite{bhattacharya2023coswara}. Raw
recordings are not redistributed. Analysis code, frozen configuration records,
and aggregate evidence tables will accompany the archived project release.
